CLAIM MAP
---------
Field 1 — What was literally measured:
The foundational 2010 experiment compared brief expansive and contractive poses
and measured immediate self-reported power, one gambling choice, and salivary
testosterone and cortisol in a small laboratory sample.

Field 3 — Weaker defensible claim:
Expansive versus contractive body positions may increase immediate self-reported
feelings of power or related self-evaluations. Evidence for hormonal change,
robust behavioral improvement, and real-world benefits is absent, inconsistent,
or sensitive to design and bias.

Field 6 — Claim under audit:
The post-2015 power-posing literature is an updating, contested evidence network
rather than a review-hub-laundered consensus: independent replications and
methodological critiques materially narrowed the surviving claim mainly to
self-reported outcomes.

Target layer:
Downstream citation and synthesis.

SOURCES
-------
P1 | 2010 | Carney, Cuddy & Yap — Power Posing | primary experiment | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797610383437
    Bounded claim: Two brief expansive poses caused higher felt power, risk tolerance, increased testosterone, and decreased cortisol, with actionable real-world implications.
P2 | 2015 | Ranehill et al. — Assessing the Robustness of Power Posing | large replication | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797614553946
    Bounded claim: Replicated increased self-reported power but found no effects on hormones or risk tolerance.
P3 | 2015 | Carney, Cuddy & Yap — Review and Summary | author review | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797614566855
    Bounded claim: Reviewed 33 experiments and argued that expansive versus contractive displays had broad embodied effects while treating the Ranehill divergence as potentially methodological.
P4 | 2016 | Garrison, Tang & Schmeichel — Embodying Power | preregistered replication | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1948550616652209
    Bounded claim: Failed to find effects on risk taking or subjective power in a preregistered replication and extension.
P5 | 2017 | Simmons & Simonsohn — P-Curving the Evidence | methodological critique | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797616658563
    Bounded claim: A p-curve of the 33-paper review did not show evidential value for the broad published effect set.
P6 | 2017 | Gronau et al. — Bayesian Meta-Analysis of Felt Power | preregistered-study meta-analysis | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1080/23743603.2017.1326760
    Bounded claim: Six preregistered studies provided strong evidence for a felt-power effect, reduced to moderate evidence among participants unfamiliar with the hypothesis.
P7 | 2018 | Cuddy, Schultz & Fosse — More Comprehensive P-Curve | reply / synthesis | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797617746749
    Bounded claim: A broader 55-study p-curve showed evidential value for emotions, felt power, and self-evaluations, contesting the earlier p-curve's study selection.
P8 | 2018 | Cesario & Johnson — Power Poseur | ecologically oriented primary tests | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1948550617725153
    Bounded claim: Four dyadic studies found nearly uniform null effects on behavioral and cognitive outcomes, including negotiation.
P9 | 2020 | Körner & Schütz — Dominance or Prestige? | independent review | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1111/spc3.12559
    Bounded claim: No clear evidence for short-term physiological or behavioral effects, but repeated self-perception effects; proposed separating expansive poses from upright posture.
P10 | 2020 | Elkjær et al. — Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis | systematic review / meta-analysis | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1745691620919358
    Bounded claim: Across 73 studies, affective and behavioral differences were driven mainly by contractive-versus-neutral contrasts, not expansive-versus-neutral contrasts.
P11 | 2021 | Körner, Röseler & Schütz — Commentary on Elkjær et al. | methodological commentary | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1745691620984474
    Bounded claim: Raised preregistration, study-selection, and theoretical-classification constraints on the Elkjær meta-analysis.
P12 | 2022 | Körner et al. — Dominance and Prestige Meta-Analysis | preregistered meta-analysis | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1037/bul0000356
    Bounded claim: Across 88 studies and 9,779 participants, self-report and some behavioral effects appeared, physiological effects did not, and behavioral effects were sensitive to publication bias, outliers, and demand characteristics.
