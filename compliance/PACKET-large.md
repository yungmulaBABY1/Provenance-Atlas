LARGE SUPPLIED POWER-POSING NETWORK
===================================

PURPOSE
-------
This is a large supplied-packet execution and burden test. It is not an
open-ended retrieval test and not a pre-specified substantive correctness oracle.

BOUNDARY
--------
Use only the 26 supplied sources and 70 selected load-bearing citation edges.
Do not add sources or search for new sources during execution.

The edge list is deliberately bounded rather than exhaustive. Citation counts
mean bounded in-degree within this packet, not external database totals.

CLAIM MAP
---------
Field 1 — Literally measured in the foundational source:
A small laboratory experiment compared brief expansive and contractive poses and
measured immediate self-reported power, one risk choice, testosterone and cortisol.

Field 3 — Weaker defensible claim:
Body-position manipulations may produce small self-reported or affective changes
under some conditions. Hormonal effects are not robust; broad behavioral and
real-world benefits are inconsistent, outcome-specific and sensitive to controls,
demand and context.

Field 6 — Claim under audit:
The post-2015 citation network materially updated and narrowed the original
power-posing package rather than preserving it through a closed review-hub
consensus.

Target layer:
Downstream citation, synthesis and model updating.

SOURCES
-------
P1 | 2010 | Carney, Cuddy & Yap — Power Posing | primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797610383437
    Bounded claim: Brief expansive poses were reported to increase felt power, risk tolerance and testosterone while decreasing cortisol, with actionable implications.
P2 | 2011 | Huang et al. — Powerful Postures Versus Powerful Roles | primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797610391912
    Bounded claim: Across three experiments, posture was reported to affect implicit power, action and abstract thinking more strongly than assigned role.
P3 | 2015 | Cuddy et al. — Preparatory Power Posing and Job Interviews | primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1037/a0038543
    Bounded claim: Preparatory expansive posing was reported to improve mock-interview performance and hireability through nonverbal presence.
P4 | 2015 | Ranehill et al. — Assessing the Robustness of Power Posing | large replication | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797614553946
    Bounded claim: A larger replication found increased self-reported power but no effects on hormones or risk tolerance.
P5 | 2015 | Carney, Cuddy & Yap — Review and Summary | author review | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797614566855
    Bounded claim: An author-led review assembled 33 experiments and argued for broad embodied effects while explaining differences from the Ranehill replication.
P6 | 2016 | Garrison, Tang & Schmeichel — Embodying Power | preregistered replication | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1948550616652209
    Bounded claim: A preregistered replication found no gambling effect and no predicted increase in subjective power.
P7 | 2017 | Smith & Apicella — Winners, Losers, and Posers | primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1016/j.yhbeh.2016.11.003
    Bounded claim: The study found no main pose effect on testosterone, cortisol, felt power or risk taking, while reporting a competition-outcome interaction.
P8 | 2017 | Simmons & Simonsohn — P-Curving the Evidence | methodological critique | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797616658563
    Bounded claim: A p-curve analysis of the author review's study set concluded that the broad evidence set lacked evidential value.
P9 | 2017 | Gronau et al. — Bayesian Meta-Analysis of Felt Power | preregistered-study meta-analysis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1080/23743603.2017.1326760
    Bounded claim: Six preregistered studies supported a small felt-power effect, with evidence weakening among participants unfamiliar with the hypothesis.
P10 | 2017 | Credé & Phillips — Revisiting the Power Pose Effect | multiverse reanalysis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1948550617714584
    Bounded claim: Hormonal inferences in the original study were highly sensitive to defensible analytic specifications.
P11 | 2018 | Cuddy, Schultz & Fosse — More Comprehensive P-Curve | reply / synthesis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/0956797617746749
    Bounded claim: A broader p-curve was reported to show evidential value for emotions, felt power and self-evaluations, contesting the earlier study selection.
P12 | 2018 | Cesario & Johnson — Power Poseur | ecologically oriented direct tests | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1948550617725153
    Bounded claim: Four dyadic studies reported nearly uniform null effects on behavior and negotiation despite manipulation checks and occasional felt-power differences.
P13 | 2019 | Metzler & Grèzes — Repeated Power Postures and Hormones | repeated-manipulation experiment | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.7717/peerj.6726
    Bounded claim: Repeated expansive or contractive postures did not affect testosterone, cortisol or progesterone.
P14 | 2019 | Chen et al. — Powerful Postures Do Not Lead to Risky Behaviors | replication / preregistered replication | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1017/prp.2019.17
    Bounded claim: Two experiments, including a preregistered Bayesian replication, found no reliable effect on risk taking and no replicated felt-power effect.
P15 | 2020 | Körner & Schütz — Dominance or Prestige? | independent review | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1111/spc3.12559
    Bounded claim: The review found no clear short-term physiological or behavioral effect, more repeatable self-perception effects and a need to distinguish poses from upright postures.
P16 | 2020 | Elkjær et al. — Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis | systematic review / meta-analysis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1745691620919358
    Bounded claim: Across 73 studies, affective and behavioral differences were driven mainly by contractive-versus-neutral contrasts rather than expansive-versus-neutral effects; hormonal evidence was not supported.
P17 | 2021 | Körner, Petersen & Schütz — Body Postures and Self-Worth | primary experiments | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1007/s12144-019-00371-1
    Bounded claim: Expansive versus contractive postures were reported to affect state self-esteem, with design attention to hypothesis awareness and demand.
P18 | 2022 | Körner, Röseler & Schütz — Commentary on Elkjær et al. | methodological commentary | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1177/1745691620984474
    Bounded claim: The commentary raised preregistration, missing-study, study-selection and theoretical-classification constraints on the 2020 meta-analysis.
P19 | 2022 | Körner et al. — Dominance and Prestige Meta-Analysis | preregistered meta-analysis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1037/bul0000356
    Bounded claim: Across 88 studies, self-report and some behavioral effects appeared, physiological effects did not, and behavioral results were sensitive to bias, outliers and demand characteristics.
P20 | 2022 | Straub et al. — Body Posture and the Stroop Effect | conceptual replications / meta-analysis | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1016/j.actpsy.2022.103497
    Bounded claim: Two conceptual replications and a meta-analysis questioned whether posture reliably modulates Stroop interference.
P21 | 2023 | Elkjær et al. — Bodily Displays in a Personally Relevant Task | neutral-control primary experiment | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1002/brb3.2855
    Bounded claim: Expansive displays produced some more favorable emotions and approach tendencies than neutral or contractive conditions, while testing personally relevant action.
P22 | 2023 | Metzler et al. — Approach and Avoidance Under Social Threat | primary experiment | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0286904
    Bounded claim: Brief power-related poses were reported to alter approach and avoidance decisions in response to social-threat signals.
P23 | 2024 | Mikkelsen et al. — Bodies in Action | active/passive-control primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1111/sjop.13046
    Bounded claim: Body displays produced small nonsignificant effects on adaptive behavior, with some favorable affective differences for expansive versus contractive displays.
P24 | 2024 | Barel et al. — Neuroendocrine Levels and Risk-Taking | hormonal replication / extension | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1186/s40359-024-02194-7
    Bounded claim: The original testosterone and felt-power results did not replicate; cortisol and risk findings were partial and hormonal-subgroup effects were exploratory.
P25 | 2025 | Mikkelsen et al. — The Power of Posing in Daily Life | daily-life behavioral study | FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1002/brb3.70643
    Bounded claim: Body-display instructions did not consistently affect taking personally relevant action or associated affect in daily life.
P26 | 2025 | Wainio-Theberge et al. — Physical Mechanisms of Postural Feedback | mechanism-focused primary experiment | ABSTRACT-CONFIRMED | DOI 10.1111/psyp.70095
    Bounded claim: Variation in neck flexion and subjective difficulty helped explain subtle mood effects, with effects moderated by body awareness.
