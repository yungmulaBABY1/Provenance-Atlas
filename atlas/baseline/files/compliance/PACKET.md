FIELD / SUBFIELD

Social-psychological research on expansive versus contractive “power posing.”

CENTRAL CONTESTED CLAIM

The cited literature independently establishes that briefly adopting expansive
power poses produces reliable behavioral and physiological benefits.

ADJACENT FIELDS

Meta-science, publication-bias analysis, replication methodology, psychophysiology,
and measurement theory.

KNOWN CONTRARY EVIDENCE / CONCERNS

- S2 is a larger conceptual replication that reports no hormonal or behavioral
  effects while reproducing the self-reported feeling-of-power effect.
- S3 responds by reviewing 33 published experiments and proposing potential
  moderators, while also stating that direct replications are needed.
- S4 applies p-curve to the studies assembled by S3 and concludes that the
  published evidence does not show evidential value for behavioral or
  physiological effects after accounting for selective reporting.
