==================================================

           between attribution asymmetry and source distribution)
  PART 2 — compact comparative version for the coverage-network audit (insert as

-----------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS ORGANIZED AS A TRIAD
-----------------------------------------------------------------

A piece constructs meaning through three distinct mechanisms that are easy to
collapse into a generic "word choice" finding, and should not be:

  NARRATIVE FLAGS ..... facts or labels that CLASSIFY the actors or the event.
                        What kind of person is this? What kind of event is this?

  CAUSAL BRIDGES ...... facts that explain WHY one thing followed another.
                        Their removal changes the reader's model of the sequence,
                        not merely its emotional register.

  ATMOSPHERIC CUES .... details that tell the reader HOW TO FEEL about the event
                        before the facts establish whether that feeling is
                        warranted.

SEVERITY — REBUTTABLE DEFAULT, NOT A FIXED HIERARCHY:
Default ranking is causal bridges > narrative flags > atmospheric cues, because
removing a causal bridge changes the reader's MODEL of what happened rather than
its register. A causal omission dropped into a "word choice" bucket is a diluted
finding, and this default exists to prevent that.

BUT THE DEFAULT IS REBUTTABLE. An event-classification flag ("riot," "reform,"
"crisis," "correction"), a motive attribution, a denominator choice, or a sustained
atmospheric frame can outweigh a modest causal omission. To rank one above a causal
bridge, STATE WHY — name the concrete construction effect. Rank the effect, not the
mechanism's category.

WHERE QUANTITATIVE CLAIMS FIT — baselines are not a fourth mechanism. They are the
NUMERICAL FORM of the same three:
  category redefinition / changed geographic scope .... narrative flag, in numbers
  denominator, adjustment, comparison point ........... causal bridge, in numbers
  which year or window is chosen for contrast ......... atmospheric cue, in numbers
"Unemployment rose" vs. "rose after the plant closed" and a raw count vs. a rate are
the same operation performed on different material. Section E2.3 treats them
together for convenience; do not classify them as a separate mechanism.

THIS MODULE IS NOT CRIME-SPECIFIC. The mechanisms operate identically in economic,
policy, health, education, institutional, and cultural coverage. Crime examples
appear below as ONE domain among several. If you find yourself only able to apply
this to crime stories, you are reading the examples as the definition.

E2.1 — NARRATIVE FLAGS: WHICH ATTRIBUTES ARE ACTIVATED
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Pieces construct meaning through WHICH ATTRIBUTES OF THE ACTORS OR EVENT THEY
ACTIVATE — foregrounding, delaying, routing through quotes, or omitting.

CATEGORIES OF NARRATIVE-BEARING ATTRIBUTE (all domains):

  PERSONAL / DEMOGRAPHIC
    age, race, ethnicity, sex, religion, nationality, immigration or residency
    status, family role, housing status, health status

  AFFILIATION AND INTEREST
    political party, ideological alignment, funding source, employer, industry
    ties, institutional membership, advocacy-group affiliation, prior employment
    by an interested party

  CREDENTIAL AND STANDING
    "expert" vs. "activist" vs. "critic"; "economist" vs. "industry-funded
    economist"; "researcher" vs. "campaigner"; "official" vs. "spokesperson";
    who gets a title and who gets a descriptor

  ROLE AND STATUS IN THE EVENT
    victim, perpetrator, bystander, official, whistleblower, defendant,
    complainant, beneficiary, cost-bearer

  EVENT CLASSIFICATION — often the single most consequential flag
    Is this a crime, a protest, unrest, terrorism, self-defense, activism, a
    mental-health crisis? A reform, a cut, a restructuring, a closure? An outbreak,
    a cluster, a surge, a normal seasonal variation? A recession, a slowdown, a
    correction? A shortage, a backlog, a crisis? The noun chosen is a claim.

  MOTIVE AND CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION
    stated motive, alleged motive, inferred motive; who or what is identified as
    responsible; whether the piece names a cause at all

AN ATTRIBUTE IS A CANDIDATE FINDING ONLY WHERE:
  1. it was known or credibly reported at publication time;
  2. it is materially relevant to the interpretation the piece creates;
  3. comparable actors or items are treated differently; OR
  4. the piece activates it indirectly — through quotes, labels, images, captions,
     historical context, or linked stories — while avoiding it in outlet voice.

APPOSITIONAL FLAGS — labels embedded in introductions or appositives:
  "the controversial professor," "the far-right group," "the reform-minded
  prosecutor," "the industry-backed critic," "John Smith, an election denier, said"
These arrive grammatically as SETTLED BACKGROUND rather than as claims requiring
sourcing, which is precisely what makes them effective. For each: who supplied the
classification? Is it contested? Are comparable descriptors applied to comparable
parties on the other side? An appositional label is a claim wearing the costume of
a fact.

MOTIVE AND CAUSE — EPISTEMIC LEVELS. Classify every motive or cause attribution:
  DOCUMENTED ............ manifesto, direct statement, verified communication
  OFFICIALLY ALLEGED .... law enforcement, complaint, indictment, institutional finding
  SOURCE-INFERRED ....... witness, advocate, expert, or interested party's inference
  OUTLET-INFERRED ....... the article's own synthesis
  UNKNOWN ............... evidence insufficient
THE TEST: does the piece SILENTLY MOVE BETWEEN LEVELS — an officially-alleged motive
restated later as documented, or a source's inference appearing in outlet voice?
Applies well beyond crime: why a company relocated, why voters shifted, why scores
fell, why a policy was introduced, why an institution disciplined someone.

DO NOT INFER UNKNOWN ATTRIBUTES. Never infer race, religion, immigration status,
ideology, funding, or motive from names, appearance, location, employer, or
rumor. An unconfirmed attribute is not an omission.

TREATMENT CLASSIFICATION — for each material attribute:

  ACTIVATED ....... explicitly foregrounded or repeatedly emphasized
  REPORTED ........ included proportionately as ordinary context
  DELAYED ......... included only after the dominant impression is established
  QUOTE-ROUTED .... supplied through a speaker, absent from outlet voice
  EUPHEMIZED ...... referenced indirectly through substitute language
  OMITTED ......... known, material, and absent
  INAPPLICABLE .... unknown, immaterial, or improper to report

| Attribute | Known at pub? | Treatment | First appears | Outlet voice or quote? | Material relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

ASYMMETRY TESTS:

  1. Are identifiers supplied for one principal actor but not another?
  2. Is one party INDIVIDUALIZED while another is CATEGORIZED?
       crime:     "father of three" vs. "the suspect"
       policy:    a named family with a story vs. "affected households"
       economics: a profiled small-business owner vs. "employers"
       health:    a named patient vs. "the unvaccinated"
       education: a named teacher vs. "administrators"
  3. Are DISCLOSURES applied evenly? If one source's funding, party, or prior
     employment is noted, is the opposing source's?
  4. Are credentials assigned asymmetrically — one side "experts," the other
     "critics" or "skeptics"?
     SUBSTANTIVE-EQUIVALENCE CHECK: compare standing RELEVANT TO THE CLAIM AT
     ISSUE, not general prestige. A physician may be expert on clinical treatment
     but not population epidemiology; an economist may model costs but not clinical
     outcomes. The asymmetry finding requires comparable DOMAIN RELEVANCE, not
     comparable titles. "Both have doctorates" is not the standard.
  5. Is an attribute absent from the factual account but introduced via quote,
     headline, caption, hyperlink, or related-story box?
  6. Does the piece say cause or motive is unknown in one instance while naming it
     confidently in a comparable one?

E2.2 — CAUSAL BRIDGES: SYNONYM VARIANCE vs. CAUSAL-CONTEXT LOSS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

THE MOST IMPORTANT DISTINCTION IN THIS MODULE. Do not treat all wording
differences as synonyms.

  SYNONYM / REGISTER VARIANCE — different words, substantially the same causal
  proposition:
    drew / pulled / produced a knife
    fell / declined / dropped
    protest / demonstration / rally
    cut / reduced / trimmed

  CAUSAL-CONTEXT VARIANCE — one formulation includes or removes the fact
  explaining WHY the next thing happened:

    "police used a Taser"          vs. "the Taser failed"
    "unemployment rose"            vs. "unemployment rose after the plant closed"
    "the district cut programs"    vs. "the district cut programs after the levy failed"
    "premiums increased"           vs. "premiums increased following the mandate change"
    "the company relocated"        vs. "the company relocated after the tax change"
    "cases rose in the county"     vs. "cases rose after testing was expanded"
    "the bill failed"              vs. "the bill failed after the amendment was added"
    "enrollment declined"          vs. "enrollment declined as the district redrew boundaries"

  In every pair, the shorter version is TRUE. It is not a softer synonym — it
  removes the reader's ability to model the sequence.

THREE BRIDGE TYPES — DO NOT CONVERT "AFTER" INTO "BECAUSE."
Several formulations below establish CHRONOLOGY, not causation. The term "causal
bridge" invites upgrading a temporal association into a causal fact. Classify:

  SEQUENCE BRIDGE ........... what intervened between two events
      "The levy failed; the district later cut programs."
  MECHANISM BRIDGE .......... how one event produced or constrained the next
      "The failed levy left a $12M gap, after which the district cut programs."
  ATTRIBUTED CAUSAL BRIDGE .. a source's explanation for why one caused the other
      "District officials said the failed levy forced the cuts."

A missing SEQUENCE fact can be established even where causation remains contested.
Record causal confidence SEPARATELY from whether the bridge was omitted. Omitting a
sequence fact is a finding; asserting a mechanism the evidence doesn't support is a
different finding, in the opposite direction.

| Wording used | Synonym only? | Bridge type (sequence/mechanism/attributed) | Added or removed? | Whose explanation? | Effect on reader's model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

TESTS:
  1. Does the shorter version preserve the causal relationship, or only the fact?
  2. Does it tell the reader WHAT happened while omitting WHY the next thing did?
  3. Is the missing causal fact ESTABLISHED, or is it an interested party's
     explanation? These are different findings — omitting an established causal
     fact is stronger than omitting a contested one.
  4. Is the causal fact asserted, attributed, contested, or independently visible?
  5. IS THE OMITTED BRIDGE THE ONE THAT WOULD COMPLICATE THE PIECE'S FRAME?
     A piece that omits causal bridges in both directions is thin. A piece that
     omits only the ones cutting against its frame is constructed.

SEVERITY: causal-bridge removal that materially changes the reader's model of the
sequence may be rated MODERATE or MAJOR. Synonym variance alone is MINOR at most.
DO NOT COLLAPSE THE FIRST INTO THE SECOND.

E2.3 — BASELINE AND DENOMINATOR SELECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------

A quantitative claim's meaning is set by what it is compared against. Comparison
choice is a construction lever and is largely invisible unless audited directly.

  1. RAW COUNT or RATE? Absolute numbers and per-capita rates support opposite
     impressions from identical data.
  2. WHICH BASELINE YEAR? Compared to last year, pre-pandemic, a decade ago, the
     peak, the trough? Is the chosen comparison point unusually high or low?
  3. WHICH POPULATION IS THE DENOMINATOR? Total population, affected population,
     eligible population, participants?
  4. INFLATION, SEASONAL, OR POPULATION ADJUSTMENT applied — or not?
  5. Are figures from opposing sides computed on the SAME basis?
  6. Does the piece report the comparison it used, or present a number bare?
  7. If a trend is claimed, over what window — and does the trend survive a
     different reasonable window?

ALSO TEST — common quantitative maneuvers:
  percentage vs. percentage-POINT change; stock vs. flow; cumulative total vs.
  period-specific; nominal vs. inflation-adjusted; total vs. marginal change; mean
  vs. median; forecast vs. observed; preliminary vs. revised; absolute vs. relative
  risk; survey respondents vs. total population; eligible population vs. all
  residents; share of incidents vs. share of offenders/victims/exposures; whether
  category DEFINITIONS or GEOGRAPHIC BOUNDARIES changed across the comparison;
  start-date selection; truncated axes or visual scaling if charts appear;
  uncertainty intervals and whether revisions are noted.

DENOMINATOR SWITCHING — ask specifically: is the denominator merely UNSTATED, or
has the piece MOVED BETWEEN DENOMINATORS within a single argument? Switching
mid-argument is usually worse than consistently choosing one debatable basis,
because it makes the comparison incoherent rather than merely contestable.

| Figure cited | Baseline / denominator used | Alternative available | Consistent across the argument? | Would it change the impression? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Applies to: crime statistics, unemployment, inflation, test scores, case counts,
emissions, immigration figures, spending, polling. Wherever a number appears with
a comparison, the comparison was chosen.

E2.4 — ATMOSPHERIC CUES AND HISTORICAL SCENE-SETTING
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Context is not neutral because it is true. It can tell the reader WHAT KIND OF
EVENT this is before the current facts establish the category.

  SETTING CUES — true details supplying emotional weight, sometimes without
  causal relevance:
    crime:     "in broad daylight," "in front of witnesses," "steps from a school"
    policy:    "in a district already facing cuts," "amid record profits"
    economics: "as executives received bonuses," "in a town with one employer"
    health:    "in a county with no ICU beds," "during flu season"
    education: "in a building that failed inspection"
  For each: is it MATERIALLY relevant, EMOTIONALLY relevant, both, or neither?
  State which. A cue can be all three; the finding is when it supplies aggravation
  without causal relevance.

  HISTORICAL SCENE-SETTING — which prior episode is invoked as the relevant
  precedent, when several are available:
    | Element introduced | Where placed | Factual background or interpretive category? | Connection to current event established? |

  THE COMPETING-HISTORY TEST — the sharpest question here:
  WHAT DIFFERENT HISTORY WOULD A MIRROR PIECE HAVE INVOKED, from the same true
  record? Name it concretely.
    crime:     history of police violence / history of attacks on officers
    immigration: history of immigrant crime / history of anti-immigrant violence
    economics: history of corporate relocation / history of failed subsidies
    health:    history of institutional failure / history of public overreaction
    education: history of underfunding / history of administrative bloat

  The finding is NOT that history was invoked. It is that ONE history was
  activated to classify the event while a competing and equally available history
  was not — and that the piece did not report material differences between the
  invoked precedent and the current case.

E2.5 — VIVIDNESS AND PERSONIFICATION
-----------------------------------------------------------------

CLASSIFY VIVIDNESS BEFORE RATING IT. A vivid detail is not suspect because it is
emotional:
  EVIDENTIARY ..... detail necessary to understand what occurred
  INCIDENTAL ...... true but causally irrelevant
  SELECTIVE ....... graphic specificity for one party, clinical abstraction for the other
  SYMBOLIC ........ children, sacred sites, holidays, schools, uniforms, flags
The finding is SELECTIVE vividness or symbolic weight carrying a conclusion the
facts don't establish — not vividness itself.

| Party | Named? | Biography | Visual detail | Emotional detail | Vividness type | Group/status label | Historical association |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

  1. Who is rendered as a PERSON and who as a CATEGORY?
  2. Who receives a name, photo, age, family role, aspiration, grief?
  3. Who receives institutional, demographic, or group labels instead?
  4. Do images humanize one side and abstract the other?
  5. Does vividness substitute for evidence of cause, culpability, or category
     membership?

Human-interest detail is not improper. The finding is ASYMMETRY, or vividness
carrying a conclusion the facts do not establish.

E2.6 — REQUIRED FINDING RULES
-----------------------------------------------------------------

An attribute, causal, baseline, or context finding requires ALL of:
  - the fact meets one of the three ACCESS TIERS below;
  - it was material to the interpretation the piece creates;
  - reporting it would not violate an applicable privacy, safety, legal, or
    identification norm;
  - the audit can state HOW inclusion or omission changes interpretation.

ACCESS TIERS — a fact "available at publication" is too loose. A fact can exist in
a 900-page filing, an obscure database, a foreign-language document, or a video
posted minutes before deadline without being reasonably available. Classify:

  PIECE-KNOWN ......... the article itself proves the outlet possessed it
                        (it appears elsewhere in the piece, or the piece cites the
                        document containing it)
  SOURCE-KNOWN ........ it appears in a document or statement the article
                        demonstrably used
  GENERALLY AVAILABLE . it existed publicly, but this outlet's access is INFERRED

PIECE-KNOWN and SOURCE-KNOWN support strong selection findings. GENERALLY AVAILABLE
requires caution and an explicit statement of what makes access plausible given the
outlet's format, deadline, and resources — cap at MODERATE absent that showing.

CORPUS-REQUIRED ROUTING — where asymmetry cannot be tested among actors or outlets
within the current event, mark it CORPUS-REQUIRED rather than inferring
inconsistency. Establishing that an outlet SYSTEMATICALLY activates an attribute in
one direction — names immigration status here, does it elsewhere? discloses
industry funding but not advocacy funding? applies "terrorism" asymmetrically? —
requires comparable stories across events. State the hypothesis, state that this
audit cannot test it, and route it to a corpus audit. Do not convert an untestable
hypothesis into a finding.

MANDATORY INNOCENT EXPLANATIONS — consider each where applicable:
  identification not confirmed; cause still under investigation; legal restriction
  or minor status; privacy; retaliation risk; genuine irrelevance to the immediate
  event; newsroom policy against demographic identification absent clear relevance;
  uncertainty whether a label accurately applies; space; the reader is assumed to
  know from prior coverage.

OWNERSHIP / NON-DOUBLE-COUNTING — REQUIRED FOR AGGREGATION INTEGRITY.
This module CLASSIFIES mechanisms; it does not create a parallel set of findings.
Record each construction lever ONCE, assigned to the section that best captures its
primary effect. Cross-reference rather than multiply.

  Missing explanatory fact ............... causal bridge (NOT a second generic omission)
  Prior case assigning category membership  pattern linkage (mechanism: narrative flag)
  Lead image humanizing one party ........ packaging (mechanism: personification)
  Number misleading via denominator ...... baseline (NOT a second factual finding
                                           unless the number itself is false)
  Event-classification noun .............. narrative flag (NOT also packaging, unless
                                           the headline separately does other work)

If a lever appears in two sections, name which section OWNS it and state the other
as a cross-reference. Inflating one choice into four findings corrupts the coherence
count downstream.

SYMMETRY REQUIREMENT — state this in the output:
  Omitting an attribute can be construction. Foregrounding it can also be
  construction. Omitting a causal bridge can be construction. Asserting a contested
  cause can also be construction. The question is never whether the treatment helps
  one side. It is whether the treatment is PROPORTIONATE, EVIDENTIALLY GROUNDED,
  and CONSISTENT across comparable parties.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
D7 — NARRATIVE-FLAG AND CONTEXT VARIANCE ACROSS OUTLETS
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Attribute / context element | Outlet A | Outlet B | Outlet C | ... |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Compare across outlets, where each is materially relevant:
  demographic attributes; affiliation, funding, and interest disclosures;
  credential labels ("expert" vs. "critic"); role/status assignment; EVENT
  CLASSIFICATION (which noun each outlet chose); motive or cause attribution;
  biography and individualization; which prior episode is invoked as precedent;
  setting and atmospheric cues.

For each divergence:
  1. Was the attribute known when EACH outlet published? (Use the availability
     matrix — do not count an unconfirmed attribute as an omission.)
  2. Is the difference inclusion, omission, delay, quote-routing, or euphemism?
  3. Materially relevant, or merely inflammatory?
  4. Does one outlet individualize a party another categorizes?
  5. Does the variance correlate with headline, sequencing, or source choices?
  6. Could timing, privacy rules, confirmation standards, house style, or outlet
     policy explain it?

NETWORK-WIDE PATTERNS — RECLASSIFY, DO NOT AUTOMATICALLY DOWNGRADE.
If every outlet treats an attribute the same way, that establishes the outlet is not
an OUTLIER. It does NOT establish that the treatment is proportionate or
non-misleading. Every outlet can inherit the same official euphemism, wire framing,
legal convention, or shared blind spot.

  DOWNGRADE: any claim that this outlet is distinctive or slanted relative to peers.
  DO NOT DOWNGRADE: the underlying construction finding itself.
  RECORD SEPARATELY: a network-wide convention finding, which is often the more
  interesting result.

TEST THE SOURCE OF THE UNIFORMITY — these are different findings:
  independent editorial convergence (several newsrooms reached it separately)
  wire inheritance (one wire story, many mastheads)
  common primary source (all worked from one release or briefing)
  legal or professional convention (identification norms, sub judice rules)
  shared blind spot (nobody looked)
Only the first is evidence the convention is considered. The rest are evidence it is
inherited, which leaves the substantive question open.

ABSENCE CLUSTER: are the same material attributes OMITTED by outlets that otherwise
share a framing cluster? Shared omission can be more diagnostic than shared wording,
especially where those outlets used DIFFERENT source chains — independent omission is
a stronger signal than common-source omission. Distinguish the two explicitly.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
D8 — SYNONYM VARIANCE vs. CAUSAL-CONTEXT VARIANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

| Event element | Outlet wordings | Synonym only? | Causal context added/removed | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|

WHAT THE NETWORK ADDS — stated precisely. A single-piece audit CAN establish
causal-context loss by comparing the article against the primary material it
demonstrably used. The network does something narrower and still valuable: it shows
the alternative wording was FEASIBLE AND COMMONLY SELECTED, so the shorter
formulation was not forced by the source.

CAVEAT BUILT IN: outlets repeating the same wire sentence do not independently
demonstrate anything. Before treating cross-outlet agreement as evidence, establish
how many INDEPENDENT source chains it represents (Section B3).

  1. Which outlets include the causal bridge and which omit it?
  2. Does inclusion correlate with sequencing, sourcing, or framing cluster?
  3. CRITICAL — CHECK THE PRIMARY SOURCE, IN BOTH FORMS. A wording difference may
     be DOCUMENT DEPENDENCE rather than editorial choice: institutions often issue
     a written release AND a spoken briefing with different language, and outlets
     inherit whichever they used. Before rating a verb difference as construction,
     establish which source document each outlet worked from. (Cross-reference
     Section D2 item 2.)
  4. Rank causal-context variance ABOVE synonym variance where it changes the
     reader's model of the sequence.

-----------------------------------------------------------------
D9 — BASELINE AND DENOMINATOR VARIANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Where outlets cite figures for the same phenomenon:

| Figure | Outlet | Baseline / denominator | Raw or rate | Adjustment applied | Impression supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

  1. Do outlets use different comparison points for the same underlying data?
  2. Does the choice correlate with framing cluster?
  3. Does any outlet report the comparison basis, or do they present bare numbers?
  4. Would a common basis change the apparent disagreement between outlets?

This is often the cleanest quantitative divergence available and it is invisible
without cross-outlet comparison.
