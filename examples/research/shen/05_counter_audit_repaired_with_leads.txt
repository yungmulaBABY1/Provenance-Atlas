I. REVISED THESIS PARAGRAPH
============================================================

Shen et al. measured a legitimate but bounded dimension of game performance—
realized advancement in selected EQ2 and CR3 character/account observations—
and openly defended that operationalization while acknowledging genre, culture,
and self-selection limits. The lead-informed rerun further shows that EQ2’s
official mentoring system could accelerate an apprentice while the reported
model did not isolate event-level help, and that CR3 measured login-session
seconds without a reported action-input/AFK filter. EQ2 also conditions level
on all character play seconds, so fishing, gathering, socializing, and other
non-leveling activity remain inside the denominator. The paper anticipates that
men and women may pursue different goals and cites external survey evidence for
greater average male achievement motivation and greater average female social
motivation. That evidence makes the paper's preferred direction plausible, but
it does not observe actual activity allocation or advancement-directed time in
Shen's analytic samples. The same unmeasured mechanism permits the live mirror
that selection or concentration on the visible leveling metric could bias the
comparison toward parity and mask a difference among genuine maximizers. E0
therefore classifies the defense DIRECTIONALLY-CONTESTED and leaves CV-7 at its
pre-E0 severity. Those facts narrow the construct to
socially situated advancement per total recorded game time; because prevalence,
magnitude, and gender differentials were not established, they do not prove
bias or add an independent major lever. The paper nevertheless overstates its
own results when it concludes that there is no significant gender-based
performance disparity: its
reported EQ2 and CR3 models contain significant sex-dependent interactions,
including a level-dependent reversal in CR3, and the remaining nonsignificant
coefficients were not tested against justified equivalence bounds. Related
field studies in League of Legends and World of Tanks, whose bounded reception
checks found no formal correction or rebuttal but which are partly same-author
and do not run the decisive equivalence test, support only the narrower claim
that a consistent male advantage is absent across several game-specific
progression/XP outcomes after play or experience is considered. The citation
network is not functionally closed, although specific checked papers mutate
two-MMO advancement into general gaming skill or esports ability; because no
direct critique with verified survival and low uptake was found, suppression is
not established. A decisive test would independently preregister representative
entry cohorts, comparable multidimensional outcomes, selection/attrition
analyses, level- and context-specific contrasts, confidence intervals, and
justified equivalence margins across multiple games.

Lead 4 is not used in this paragraph. It remains filed and unexercised pending
an actual citation-network rerun.
