1. WHAT WAS LITERALLY MEASURED
============================================================

Study I — EverQuest II (EQ2)
----------------------------

- Data source and period: Sony Online Entertainment server records from 2006.
  Player variables were a September 2006 snapshot; activity logs covered
  January–September 2006. The analysis included characters active at least once
  on the Guk PvE server during September 4–10, 2006.
- Population/sample: the paper reports 9,483 active players in the selected
  week; descriptive/model Ns vary with available variables and the split
  between guilded and nonguilded characters. In the descriptive table, gender
  information was available for 6,448 male and 1,513 female players for several
  measures. Women were 18% of the active sample.
- Gender variable: voluntarily self-reported demographic gender at profile
  registration, coded dichotomously (male = 1).
- Outcomes: attained character level and attained tradeskill level, with all
  level-70 cases removed because capped characters could not progress further.
- Main conditioning variable: accumulated character play time from character
  creation through September 10, 2006.
- Model: linear mixed-effects models. Fixed effects were gender, total play
  time, and gender × total-play-time. Random effects were character class,
  account ID, and guild ID where applicable. Models were run separately for
  guilded and nonguilded characters and for character and tradeskill level.
- Unit of analysis: character observations nested in player accounts; guild and
  class were additional random effects. The paper often calls these “players,”
  but explicitly states that most variables are at character level and that an
  account may contain multiple characters.
- Operational performance rule: at equal accumulated play time, the character
  at the higher character/tradeskill level is treated as having advanced faster.
  This is an inferred conditional slope from an attained-level snapshot, not a
  directly observed within-character progression trajectory.

Study II — Chevaliers’ Romance III (CR3)
----------------------------------------

- Data source and period: a five-week website survey in late 2011 plus partial
  behavioral logs from selected servers for May–September 2010 and
  December 2011–June 2012.
- Population/sample: about 18,000 valid survey responses; the main linked
  behavioral sample was about 2,000 respondents (reported descriptively as
  1,639 men and 545 women for minimum/maximum level). The model used 90,495
  level-up event observations. Women were 23.34% of the linked sample.
- Gender variable: survey self-report, coded dichotomously (male = 1).
- Outcome: logged-in seconds between a character reaching level n and level
  n+1, derived algorithmically from login, logout, and level-up timestamps.
- Model: linear mixed-effects models predicting time to level up. Fixed effects
  were gender and new level, with a second model adding gender × new-level.
  Random effects were character ID nested in account ID. Character class and
  guild membership were unavailable and therefore not modeled.
- Unit of analysis: repeated level-up events nested in characters nested in
  accounts.
- Operational performance rule: at the same new level, the character requiring
  fewer logged-in seconds to reach that level is treated as performing better.

Definition–operationalization check
-----------------------------------

Central named construct: game performance.

Source’s explicit operational definition (Theory and Prior Research, final
paragraphs before Study I):

  “We operationalize game performance as a function of character advancement
  (leveling) and voluntary play time …”

The two study-specific rules are then stated as: (a) higher EQ2 level at equal
total play time and (b) less CR3 time from level n to n+1.

Actual items/coding rules retrieved:

1. EQ2 attained character level conditional on total character play time.
2. EQ2 attained tradeskill level conditional on total character play time.
3. CR3 logged-in seconds between successive level-up events conditional on the
   new level reached.

Operationalization retrieval basis: ITEMS-RETRIEVED.

Operationalization scope result: coextensive with the paper’s explicit narrow
definition “speed of character advancement,” but narrower than the paper’s
unqualified later wording “game performance” or “performance disparity.” The
map therefore preserves both layers rather than pretending the label and the
operational definition are identical in scope.

No definitional-scope-creep subtype is entered at this map stage because the
actual coding rules do track the stated speed-of-advancement definition. The
construct prosecutor must separately decide whether substituting that defined
measure for broader game performance is defensible.

Construct-establishment check
-----------------------------

TRIGGERED. The paper does more than report advancement associations. It states
that a stereotype is false and “a potential cause for unequal participation,”
and it describes contextual/cultural factors as the likely explanation. Those
are wrongness and causal/harm claims.

- PREVALENCE — UNKNOWN. The introduction reports that women are perceived as
  inferior and cites gender/gaming literature, but neither this paper nor the
  checked cited abstracts supplied a prevalence estimate for endorsement of the
  specific belief “women are inferior players.” A full targeted prevalence
  review was not performed at this stage.
- FORM — UNKNOWN. Retrieved text does not establish whether the target belief is
  a coherent strong claim about general ability, a weak average-difference
  belief, a genre-specific belief, or scattered context-dependent judgments.
- DOMAIN — ESTABLISHED-REPORTED. The paper’s empirical domain is advancement in
  EQ2 and CR3. The stereotype language expands to female players/gaming in
  general, while the limitations explicitly say other genres, including more
  challenging esports games, may or may not follow the observed pattern.
- WRONGNESS — ESTABLISHED-REPORTED for the narrow study-sample belief that women
  advance more slowly under the fitted models; UNKNOWN for a domain-general
  belief about women’s gaming ability or performance.
- HARM — ESTABLISHED-CITED, coarse map status only. The paper cites Chan (2008),
  Vermeulen et al. (2014), and Ratan et al. (2015) for competence, stress,
  stereotype-threat, and STEM-perception pathways. The checked Chan abstract
  reports effects in the predicted direction but no statistically significant
  differences; the retrieved Ratan conference description concerns gendered
  gaming stereotypes and STEM perceptions. Whether this support is contested
  or strong enough for the paper’s causal language is deliberately left to the
  construct prosecutor.

2. STRONGEST CLAIM THE SOURCE ITSELF MAKES
============================================================

The strongest central performance claim appears in “Gender Performance Gap
Debunked”:

  “The rigor of our method allows us to debunk the gender gap with confidence:
  Based on our data from two MMOs, there is no significant gender-based
  performance disparity.”

The paper then makes two stronger interpretive claims:

- Abstract/conclusion: perceived performance disparities “seem to result” from
  factors confounded with gender, not gender itself.
- Abstract/conclusion: “The stereotype of female players as inferior is not
  only false, but it is also a potential cause for unequal participation in
  digital gaming.”

The hedges are preserved: “based on our data from two MMOs,” “seem,” and
“potential.” The paper is less hedged when it calls the answer “indisputably
negative,” says “controlling for all factors,” and says the former explanation
(inability/innate differences) is false while contextual explanations are
likely true.

Verification: FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED.


3. WEAKER DEFENSIBLE CLAIM THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SUPPORTS
============================================================

Among the selected, self-selected EQ2 and CR3 character/account observations
available in the stated periods, dichotomously self-reported male status did not
confer a consistent advancement-speed advantage under the authors’ fitted
mixed-effects models: EQ2 character/tradeskill level conditional on accumulated
play time, and CR3 logged-in time to the next level conditional on new level.

This reconstruction tracks the ACTUAL ITEMS: attained character/tradeskill
levels and time between level-up events. It does not substitute “general gaming
skill,” competitive success, psychomotor ability, strategic performance, or
participation causation for those items.

Description: author reconstruction from Field 1.

Selection rationale

only one claim keeps the three prosecutors aligned. The causal stereotype/
