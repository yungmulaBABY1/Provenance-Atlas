I. REVISED THESIS PARAGRAPH
============================================================

Shen et al. measured a legitimate but bounded dimension of game performance—
advancement in selected EQ2 and CR3 character/account observations—and the
paper openly explains that choice and acknowledges genre and sex-specific
selection limits. Related field studies in League of Legends and World of Tanks,
whose retrieved reception revealed no formal correction or rebuttal but which
share authors and do not run equivalence tests, support the narrower conclusion
that an unadjusted male advantage is not stable across several game-specific
progression/XP outcomes once play or experience is considered. They do not
establish the paper's unqualified “no significant gender-based performance
disparity” formulation across performance dimensions or player populations.
The citation record is not functionally closed, yet specific checked sources
mutate two-MMO advancement into general gaming skill or esports ability; no
verified direct critique was found whose low uptake could support suppression.
The decisive test is a preregistered, selection-aware, independent multi-game
study using comparable rank/win/progression outcomes, level- and context-specific
contrasts, confidence intervals, and justified equivalence margins.
