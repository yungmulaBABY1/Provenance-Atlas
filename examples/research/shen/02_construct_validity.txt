2. MEASUREMENT EXTRACTION
============================================================

Study I — EQ2
-------------

- Sample/recruitment: naturally occurring game-server users, not recruited;
  only characters active on the Guk PvE server during September 4–10, 2006.
  The paper reports 9,483 active players; the main descriptive variables include
  6,448 male and 1,513 female observations, with model Ns varying by guild status
  and outcome.
- Time scale: accumulated play since character creation through September 10,
  combined with a September snapshot; activity data existed for January–
  September but the key level/play-time variables were snapshot aggregates.
- Outcomes: character level and tradeskill level, excluding level-70 cases.
- Fixed effects: gender, total play time, gender × play time.
- Random effects: class, account ID, and guild ID where applicable.
- Proxy rule: higher attained level at the same accumulated play time equals
  better/faster performance.

Study II — CR3
--------------

- Sample/recruitment: voluntary website survey with a desirable in-game weapon
  incentive, linked where possible to partial logs from selected servers and
  noncontiguous periods; roughly 2,000 linked respondents, 90,495 level-up events.
- Time scale: logged-in seconds from one level-up event to the next.
- Outcome: time to level up from n to n+1.
- Fixed effects: gender, new level, and in Model 2 gender × new level.
- Random effects: character ID nested in account ID.
- Proxy rule: less logged-in time to the next level equals better performance.

What was not measured
---------------------

- a common direct performance outcome across both games;
- competitive rank, win/loss, PvP success, task success under equal conditions,
  decision quality, combat efficiency, accuracy, or coordinated-team output;
- prior gaming/MMO experience as a measured covariate;
- actual social assistance, team composition, grouping exposure, mentoring, or
  resource transfers, despite EQ2’s explicitly collaborative architecture;
- motivation and whether advancement was the player’s own goal;
- entry cohort, dropout/attrition process, or performance of people who never
  entered or left before the observation window;
- a measure permitting a domain-general inference about gaming ability.

Direct measure or proxy?
------------------------

The data directly measure character advancement outcomes. They are close,
behavioral measures of advancement efficiency. They are a proxy for the broader
construct “game performance,” because performance in a multi-activity MMO is not
identical to time-conditioned leveling and tradeskill progression.

Definition–operationalization check
-----------------------------------

Explicit definition:

  “We operationalize game performance as a function of character advancement
  (leveling) and voluntary play time …”

Actual retrieved coding rules:

1. EQ2 attained character level at equal accumulated play time.
2. EQ2 attained tradeskill level at equal accumulated play time.
3. CR3 logged-in seconds from level n to n+1 at equal new level.

Operationalization retrieval basis: ITEMS-RETRIEVED.

Operationalization scope result: coextensive with the paper’s explicit narrow
definition “speed of character advancement.” It is narrower than the later
unqualified construct label “performance disparity.”

Definition-operationalization mismatch: NOT FIRED. The actual items track the
stated advancement definition. The failure is instead the move from a narrow,
explicitly defined proxy to a broader claimed construct. The actual items—not
the broad word “performance”—control the defensible claim.

Operationalization pathologization: NOT FIRED. No belief scale or viewpoint is
scored.

Grade-before-assert
-------------------

- “Women advance more slowly in these fitted study samples”: ESTABLISHED-FALSE
  within the retrieved model/sample boundaries; P2 STRONG.
- “Women are inferior gamers generally”: no stable domain-specific subclaim was
  measured or externally graded here; P2 VOID. No domain-general wrongness
  finding is allowed to anchor this audit.

4. LEVEL-OF-ANALYSIS CHECK
============================================================

Evidence level
--------------

- naturalistic behavioral/administrative game logs;
- character and repeated level-up events nested in accounts;
- one active-server snapshot/conditional model in EQ2;
- a voluntarily surveyed, partially linkable selected-server sample in CR3.

Claim level
-----------

- a gender-group claim about “performance disparity” in two MMOs;
- elsewhere in the paper, broader claims about women players, gaming generally,
  and causal participation mechanisms.

Required bridge assumptions
---------------------------

1. Character advancement efficiency represents the relevant performance
   construct.
2. Characters/accounts remaining active or linkable are sufficiently
   representative of the sex/gender groups whose performance is described.
3. Advancement goals and routes are comparable enough across gender that
   conditional leveling reflects ability rather than goal selection or social
   organization.
4. EQ2’s cross-character level-by-time relation represents individual
   progression speed.

These assumptions are partly argued and partly acknowledged, not fully tested.
A domain expert could accept advancement as an important MMO outcome while
rejecting its use as the exhaustive meaning of performance.

Verdict: ecologically valid only for a narrower claim—advancement behavior among

7. DECISIVE-TEST CHECK
============================================================

A decisive test would measure multiple pre-registered, game-native performance
dimensions rather than leveling alone; follow representative entrant cohorts
prospectively through advancement and attrition; measure prior experience,
goals, input/device familiarity, class/role, team support, and time exposure;
and compare within-person trajectories as well as end-state outcomes.

- Population: representative new entrants and continuing players in both games,
  stratified by sex/gender and sampled before survival/retention can select the
  observed group.
- Outcomes supporting the source: no consistent male advantage across direct,
  pre-specified task, combat/competitive where applicable, advancement, and
  coordinated-team outcomes after measuring exposure and selection.
- Outcomes weakening the source: stable domain-specific male advantages on
  direct outcomes despite comparable experience/exposure, or equality only on
  leveling while other important outcomes diverge.
- Does Shen run it? No. It runs a strong naturalistic advancement test, not a
  multidimensional performance or selection test.
- Does the paper acknowledge the gap? Partly. It acknowledges genre limits,
  self-selection, and the possibility of different goals, but still calls
  advancement the “real performance measure.”

The decisive test would be: a prospective, representative entrant-cohort study
in the two games that repeatedly measures several pre-registered game-native
performance outcomes and attrition while directly measuring experience,
exposure, goals, role/class, social support, and input familiarity.

15. PAPER-SAFE WORDING
============================================================

A. Neutral summary sentence

In selected EQ2 and CR3 samples, the reported mixed-effects models found no
consistent male advantage in character advancement speed as operationalized by
level conditional on play time or time to the next level.

B. Direct methodological critique

The paper measures advancement efficiency, not game performance generally, so
its “no gender-based performance disparity” language exceeds the retrieved
outcomes.

C. Strong adversarial version

Shen et al. do not debunk a gender gap in gaming performance; they show that
male status did not confer a consistent advantage on one progression proxy in
two selected MMO samples.

D. Decisive-test sentence

The question requires representative prospective cohorts, direct repeated
measures across several pre-specified game-performance domains, and measured
experience, selection, goals, role, support, and exposure—not leveling alone.
