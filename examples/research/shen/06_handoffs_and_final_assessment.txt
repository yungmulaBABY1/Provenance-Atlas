OVERFIT CHECK
============================================================

The overfit check did not come back “clean” in the preregistered empty-field
sense, but it did come back unstrained. The toolkit populated only fields for
which exact downstream text existed, left ATTRIBUTED ELSEWHERE and the expected

FIRST-EVER INTERNAL-VALIDITY RESULT
============================================================

The internal-validity prompt produced the most consequential unprompted finding
in the chain. Field 6 says there is “no significant” disparity, but Shen reports
a significant EQ2 non-guilded character-level interaction (p<.001) and a
significant CR3 gender×new-level interaction (p<.001) with a reported crossover
near level 15. Remaining nonsignificant coefficients were not tested against an
equivalence margin. The exact Field 6 claim is therefore INVALID as a statistical
summary, while a heterogeneous, model-specific residual remains. Replication and
publication-bias sections honestly returned unknown/provisional where retrieval
could not support more.

The new inventory was analytically useful rather than row padding. It prevented

LABEL-VERIFICATION WATCH
============================================================

Every RETRIEVED item used to anchor a finding had an actual quotation and
locator. The one initially retrieved joint-citation item lacking a stable
document locator and source-disambiguation check was marked RETRIEVED — NOT
ANCHOR-ELIGIBLE and barred from Field 5.

This exposes a wording conflict in the run preamble: it said such an item “must
be downgraded to RECALLED,” but the pinned Stage 1 instrument explicitly says,
“Do not use anchor eligibility to relabel provenance,” and directs a directly
accessed item with a missing locator to remain RETRIEVED — NOT ANCHOR-ELIGIBLE.
The run followed the immutable instrument and surfaced the conflict rather than
silently changing provenance. This is not a prompt identity collision; it is a
stale or inaccurate expectation in the run instructions relative to the pinned
instrument semantics.


WHERE THE CHAIN UNDER-REACHED
============================================================

The chain under-reached rather than over-claimed in four places:

1. A complete top-50 OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar edge graph was not retrievable,
   so citation density, mutual-citation rate, and hub dependency remain UNKNOWN.
   The citation output used a selected 15-node/19-edge packet and capped its
   topology conclusion.
2. No raw data/code, direct reanalysis, or direct independent replication was
   located. The internal audit could compare reported tables with prose, but it
   could not rerun models, recover confidence intervals, or perform equivalence
   tests.
3. No retrieved source made game-specific progression mechanics or practitioner
   knowledge a documented Shen critique. The run did not plant boosting, carries,
   AFK progression, or similar mechanisms from outside the source. That leaves a
   real practitioner/mechanics blind spot, honestly signaled rather than filled
   from memory.
4. Cross-game support is heterogeneous and partly same-author. The chain cannot
   determine a common “true” gender-performance effect across advancement,
   XP/match, score, rank, win probability, genres, skill strata, or player-entry
   stages.


FINAL SHORT ASSESSMENT
============================================================

Overfit: not empty, but not strained—real misattributions and a same-author
successor were retrieved; MBH98-specific controversy/attribution-repair fields
remained empty and no cartel was invented.

Internal validity: useful on first run—it found a verified contradiction between
the mapped blanket claim and the paper's own significant interactions, plus the
absence of an equivalence design.

Honest limitation: the chain reaches a strong narrow conclusion and visibly
signals what it cannot adjudicate. Its best combined position is that selected
game-specific advancement results do not show a consistent male advantage, but
they do not establish an unqualified absence of gender-based performance
disparity; the paper's own heterogeneity makes that exact statement untenable,
and the missing independent, selection-aware, multi-game equivalence test remains
decisive.
