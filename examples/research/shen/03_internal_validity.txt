FIRST-RUN DIAGNOSTIC
============================================================

This prompt produced a non-generic, load-bearing result. The exact Field 6
wording says there is “no significant” disparity, but the paper itself reports
significant gender-dependent disparities:

1. EQ2 Table 3: the play-time × male interaction for character level among
   non-guilded characters is beta = -0.008, p < .001 (the paper interprets the
   slope as women leveling faster there).
2. CR3 Table 5: new level × male is beta = -8.289, p < .001. The paper says the
   fitted gender difference changes direction at approximately level 15, after
   which men take less logged-in time per level than women.

These are not external mechanics planted into the audit. They are the paper's
reported coefficients and interpretation. They do not show one uniform male
advantage; they do defeat the literal blanket statement that there is no
statistically significant gender-based disparity. The equality/no-disparity
language also rests partly on nonsignificant coefficients without confidence
intervals, an equivalence margin, or an equivalence/noninferiority test.


SECTION A: DATA GENERATION AUDIT
============================================================

A1. Collection and design
-------------------------

EQ2 is an observational analysis of Sony Online Entertainment administrative
logs plus account-level demographics. The population was the Guk player-versus-
environment server, and “active” meant playing at least once during September
4–10, 2006. Outcomes and covariates were drawn from a September snapshot and
January–September activity logs. The paper reports 9,483 active players; the
analysis uses substantially fewer observations where variables are complete.
Gender was dichotomous and self-reported.

CR3 combines a late-2011 online survey (about 18,000 valid respondents) with
partial administrative logs from selected servers and noncontiguous periods
(May–September 2010 and December 2011–June 2012). Roughly 2,000 respondents
could be linked; the multilevel model uses 90,495 level-up events. Gender was
dichotomous and self-reported.

Both studies are observational. The central fitted associations are generally
described as conditional comparisons, but the discussion's explanation that
apparent disparity is confounded by play time and motivation approaches causal
language not identified by random assignment.

A2. Selection
-------------

EQ2 selects one server, current active characters, and surviving accounts. It
does not represent people who never entered, left before the active window, or
played on other servers/games. CR3 first selects voluntary survey respondents,
then those on log-covered servers/time windows who supplied linkable account
information. The paper itself notes severe gender imbalance and the possibility
that women who self-select into these games are unusually experienced or strong
players. That is a directional threat to population-level equality claims.

A3. Missingness
---------------

EQ2 reports different Ns across descriptives/models but does not give a complete
missing-data flow or an MCAR/MAR/MNAR analysis. CR3's link from about 18,000
valid responses to roughly 2,000 logged accounts is a large observed attrition/
coverage step. The paper compares the linked sample with the full survey mainly
on gender proportion, which is insufficient to establish ignorable missingness
for performance-related traits. Missingness mechanism is therefore unknown and
plausibly related to server, tenure, survey completion, linkage, or activity.

A4. Incentives
--------------

The paper reports academic research using company-provided behavioral records;
no result-contingent sponsor incentive is reported. The platform's logging and
server-coverage decisions nonetheless determine what is observable. Incentive
risk is low-to-moderate, while measurement/coverage dependence on the platform
is material.

A5. AI-specific checks
----------------------

N/A to the 2016 analysis: no AI-assisted analysis is reported. For this audit,
the adversarial check was run against the retrieved tables and prose. It
surfaced the mismatch between the blanket “no significant” sentence and the
significant interactions.

SECTION C: EFFECT SIZE AND STATISTICAL POWER
============================================================

The paper reports unstandardized mixed-model coefficients and p-values, not a
single standardized effect size, confidence interval, smallest effect size of
interest, or prospective power calculation.

EQ2 Table 3 reports play-time × male interactions of approximately -0.001
(p=.059) for guilded character level, -0.008 (p<.001) for non-guilded character
level, -0.001 (p=.135) for guilded tradeskill level, and 0.0002 (p=.871) for
non-guilded tradeskill level. The coefficient units depend on the paper's play-
time scale, so practical magnitude cannot be recovered from p-values alone.

CR3 Table 5 reports male = -57.419 seconds (p=.484) in the no-interaction model;
with the interaction, male = 121.815 seconds (p=.194) and new level × male =
-8.289 seconds per level (p<.001). Because the interaction model's main male
coefficient is the contrast when new level equals zero, outside the observed
leveling range, it is not an average gender difference. The meaningful gender
contrast is level-specific. The authors report a crossover near level 15.

Large event counts make some slope differences detectable, but 90,495 events
are clustered within roughly 2,000 respondents/characters; events are not
90,495 independent people. Power for rejecting exact zero is not power for
establishing practical equivalence. With no equivalence margin and no intervals,
the “no disparity” inference has no reported power target.

| Reported effect size | Field typical range | Corrected meta-analysis estimate | Sample size | Estimated power | Winner's curse risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unstandardized coefficients above; no standardized effect or CI | Not established from retrieved evidence | No directly applicable meta-analysis located | EQ2: 9,483 active before analysis-specific missingness; reported descriptive/model Ns smaller. CR3: ~2,000 linked, 90,495 clustered events | Cannot calculate from reporting; likely high for some model coefficients, unknown for equivalence/practical thresholds | Low for the p<.001 interaction's sign within this specification; cannot assess magnitude; equality claim not power-defined |

Best current effect estimate: there is no defensible single cross-game “true
effect” estimate. The retrieved studies report heterogeneous level-, guild-,
outcome-, and game-specific coefficients. The most direct bounded estimate is
the reported set above, not a pooled zero.

- Internal-validity grade: E (Invalid for the exact Field 6 claim).

The grade does not mean the logged observations are fabricated or that every
coefficient is unreal. It means the adequately verified analysis as reported
DEFEATS the exact blanket claim selected upstream. A narrower, heterogeneous
set of conditional results remains usable.

1. Is the foundational finding likely real?
   The reported within-model coefficients are likely real as descriptions of
   the fitted samples, but the singular “no significant disparity” finding is
   not a faithful statistical summary of them.

2. Is its magnitude sufficient for the downstream claim?
   No cross-game equality magnitude was estimated. Practical sufficiency is
   undefined without intervals and an equivalence margin.

3. Best current estimate of the true effect size?
   No single defensible estimate. Use the model-specific coefficients reported
   in Section C and retain their guild/level heterogeneity.

4. What would upgrade the grade?
   A prospective, public analysis plan; representative or transparently
   weighted sampling; complete flow/missingness accounting; centered,
   level-specific contrasts with confidence intervals; a justified equivalence
   margin and equivalence tests; robustness across survival/nonlinear models;
   and direct independent replication with accessible code/results.

5. What decisive test has not been run?
   A preregistered multi-game equivalence design that estimates gender contrasts
   at meaningful levels/subgroups with confidence intervals and tests them
   against a justified practically negligible margin, while addressing sample
   selection and clustered longitudinal dependence.

6. Did this audit's adversarial check surface failures missed by the original
   summary?
   Yes. It placed the exact Field 6 sentence beside Tables 3 and 5 and found the
