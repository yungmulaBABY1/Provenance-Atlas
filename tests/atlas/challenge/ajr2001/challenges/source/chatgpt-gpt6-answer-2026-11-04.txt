Item 2 — DIRECT_ADJUDICATION_SUBSTANCE

CONDITION_MET_WITH_QUALIFICATION

The two papers contain genuine empirical challenges to the broad institutional explanation. However, the classifications require qualification, especially for the settler-human-capital mechanism.

Sachs (2003) — R1: Direct geography/disease channel

Question	Assessment
2a. Direct test?	CONDITION_MET
2b. Result accurately reported?	CONDITION_MET
2c. Required revision follows?	CONDITION_MET_WITH_QUALIFICATION

2a. Direct test

Sachs explicitly tests whether malaria risk explains cross-country income differences after accounting for measured institutional quality.

He uses two-stage least squares, with ecological and geographic instruments for malaria risk and institutional quality. This is not simply a theoretical alternative; it is an empirical test of the institutions-only proposition.

2b. Actual result

The source reports statistically significant coefficients for both institutional quality and malaria risk across its tested specifications, and rejects the hypothesis that geography has no additional explanatory effect.

That agrees with the audit’s representation of SA-E1. The NBER working paper confirms the study design and reported result. final.doc

2c. Required revision

The proposed revision is reasonable in direction:

Institutions are a major channel; a direct disease channel is not excluded.

But I would qualify the first half.

Sachs finds an institutional coefficient alongside the malaria effect, but this does not independently establish the general magnitude of the institutional channel.

I would prefer:

The evidence is consistent with an institutional channel, but a direct disease/geography channel remains plausible. Their relative contributions are not settled by these results.

This directly distinguishes the demonstrated regression result from a broader causal conclusion.

The result also targets a stronger claim about institutional primacy, not the literal reproducibility of AJR’s original 0.94 estimate.

Glaeser et al. (2004) — R2: Settler human capital

Question	Assessment
2a. Direct test?	CONDITION_MET_WITH_QUALIFICATION
2b. Result accurately reported?	CONDITION_MET_WITH_QUALIFICATION
2c. Required revision follows?	CONDITION_MET_WITH_QUALIFICATION

2a. Direct test

Glaeser and colleagues conduct empirical tests relevant to the human-capital explanation. They show that settler-mortality instruments predict schooling as well as institutional measures. Their Table 11 then estimates associations between instrumented schooling, executive constraints, and income.

But they do not directly identify the historical transfer of settlers’ human capital as the causal mechanism.

They provide evidence consistent with that explanation, rather than isolating it from competing pathways.

2b. Actual result

The audit’s summary is substantially accurate but incomplete.

* GL-E1 correctly reports their finding that malaria risk is statistically significant alongside settler mortality in regressions predicting institutional measures.
* GL-E2 only introduces the human-capital hypothesis as a question. It is not, by itself, evidence that the hypothesis was confirmed.
* The substantive empirical evidence appears later, including Table 11, where instrumented schooling is significant and executive constraints are not in the reported specifications.

The published paper supports these distinctions. epge.fgv.br +1

Importantly, the authors themselves acknowledge that their instruments may operate through additional channels, and that their results do not conclusively separate those mechanisms.

2c. Required revision

The audit proposes:

Institutions or settler human capital, not separately identified.

The identification warning follows from the evidence. However, the binary framing is unnecessarily restrictive.

I would recommend:

The available instrumental-variable evidence does not separately identify the contributions of institutional quality, settler human capital, and other historically transmitted or geographical channels.

This is closer to the limits stated in Glaeser et al.’s own discussion. epge.fgv.br

There is another critical distinction: Glaeser et al. principally compare schooling with political-institution measures such as executive constraints, whereas AJR’s headline estimate uses a protection-against-expropriation index. These are not interchangeable constructs.

The challenge is relevant to AJR’s wider causal interpretation, but it does not directly falsify the numerical estimate.

2d. Two classifications or four?

Two unique sources, not four independent classifications.

The rows at 02c lines 196–197 are repeated at lines 445–446.

They should remain visible in both native tables if required by the instrument, but should count as two unique source classifications for substantive review purposes.

If future software counts each appearance independently, that would be duplicate-counting.

What would change my answer?

A successful independent replication that invalidates Sachs’s malaria coefficient would weaken the R1 classification.

For Glaeser et al., more directly identified evidence separating colonial human-capital transmission from institutional transmission could strengthen the R2 classification.

Conversely, a successful identification test showing that the relevant instruments exclusively affect income through measured institutions would weaken the competing-channel objection.

Decision: The two sources are legitimate empirical challenges to stronger interpretations of AJR. Their reported results are substantially represented correctly, but the required revisions must remain conditional and should not claim either rival explanation is established.

Item 3 — NESTING_SUBSTANCE

CONDITION_NOT_MET

The shared evidence is real, but the stated nesting rationale is insufficient to establish that no independent inferential work is lost.

3a. Do both dimensions use the same HB-E1 passage?

Yes.

The original AJR paper states that the implied Nigeria–Chile income difference is an upper bound because of measurement error.

The subsequent AJR-authored handbook chapter restates the result in stronger terms without that qualification in the cited passage.

That comparison supports the claim-mutation finding.

The review-hub finding uses the same HB-E1 passage to establish that the handbook transmitted the stronger formulation.

There is genuine shared evidence, and counting the same textual discrepancy twice would be inappropriate.

3b. Does review-hub laundering add a distinct inference?

Yes. The evidence of downstream reach is separate from the evidence of claim mutation.

Consider the distinction:

Dimension	What it investigates
Claim mutation	Did the wording or strength of the claim change?
Review-hub dissemination	How widely was the subsequent synthesis cited, including without the original study?

The citation-network audit reports that, among works published since November 4, 2021:

OpenAlex handbook citation records

1,676

Works citing the handbook

276

Also citing AJR 2001

1,400 works

Cite handbook without citing AJR 2001

83.5%

I checked the saved OpenAlex response counts: 1,676 total and 276 co-citations. The arithmetic difference is 1,400. This independently supports the reported citation-pattern calculation, not the substantive interpretation of those citations.

That information is not contained in HB-E1. It comes from a separate set of database records.

It describes the potential reach of the synthesis beyond direct citers of the original paper.

However, an equally important limitation applies:

A work citing the handbook without citing AJR 2001 has not necessarily reproduced the handbook’s stronger interpretation.

The writer might cite the handbook for another argument, critically discuss it, or have read AJR without citing it.

Consequently, the citation counts support a dissemination pattern, but not the stronger assertion that 1,400 works inherited the mutated claim without inspecting the original evidence.

Is independent inferential work actually lost?

The audit does preserve the handbook citation counts in Section F (lines 203–215), so the data are not erased from the narrative.

But the aggregation explanation at line 310 and the scorecard at line 468 justify nesting only by reference to the shared HB-E1 passage.

That does not adequately account for the separate graph-based evidence.

My recommendation is to distinguish:

* Shared textual evidence: HB-E1 is counted once as evidence of claim mutation.
* Distinct citation evidence: The 1,400/1,676 citation pattern is preserved as a separate observation of potential dissemination.
* Unverified claim propagation: Whether those citing works actually reproduced the stronger claim remains unresolved.

I would not automatically turn the dissemination result into a second independently scored risk dimension. Doing so without examining the citing passages could replace one aggregation error with another.

Rather, Atlas should preserve the distinction and mark the substantive dissemination interpretation as requiring further evidence.

What would change my answer?

I would accept the original nesting rationale if the review-hub dimension were explicitly restricted to the shared textual mutation, and its separate dissemination evidence were preserved without implying it had been adjudicated.

Alternatively, inspection of an appropriately sampled set of citing passages could establish that the handbook’s stronger claim was repeatedly reproduced. That would justify reassessing whether dissemination constitutes independent inferential work deserving its own classification.

Decision: The shared-passage criterion holds, but the current explanation does not adequately account for the distinct citation-network evidence. The complete nesting condition is therefore not met.

Final recommendation for atlas.challenge

These assessments should be stored as three attributed challenge records from a previously exposed MODEL_AGENT reviewer, not as human adjudications.

I recommend retaining Item 1 with its chronology qualification, Item 2 with explicit source-specific qualifications, and Item 3 as a challenge to the sufficiency of the nesting justification.

I would also preserve the original checker status of PASS-WITH-REVIEW. None of these decisions establishes scientific validity or invalidity of AJR’s causal hypothesis.

The distinction matters for the first public Atlas example: readers should be able to see precisely why an apparently successful audit can still contain a partly verified source lock, conditional methodological classifications, and a contested aggregation decision.

That is exactly the kind of disagreement Atlas should preserve rather than resolve by silently replacing the original output.
