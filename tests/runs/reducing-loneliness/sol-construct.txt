1. CLAIM MAP (RECEIVED)

Claim under audit (map Field 6):

  Following BLOC, emotional loneliness decreased by 0.84 points on a 0-6 scale
  (sex-adjusted estimated means 2.62 at T1 and 1.78 at T2; p<0.01).

What was literally measured (map Field 1):

  In 27 baseline and 21 post telephone interviews in a self-selected Barcelona

2. MEASUREMENT EXTRACTION
-------------------------

Study object

- Sample size: N=27 at T1; N=21 at T2.
- Population actually observed: people aged 65 or older in Barcelona who
  wanted to connect more/better, chose to participate, had internet and a
  computer/smartphone, and met the stated sensory/cognitive criteria.
- Recruitment: dissemination through nearby primary-care and older-adult
  centres; posters, pamphlets, presentations, and calls. About 500 were offered
  participation, 63 expressed interest, and 27 consented.
- Exposure: eight weekly 90-120 minute facilitator-led Zoom group sessions,
  combining community activities, peer support, cognitive-behavioural content,
  coping, reminiscence, and purpose-in-life work.
- Measurement occasions: telephone interview immediately before and after the
  eight-week program, October 2021-January 2022.
- Interviewers: two trained research-team members.
- Primary instrument: 11-item DJGLS; total 0-11, social 0-5, emotional 0-6.
- Outcome for Field 6: emotional-subscale score.
- Model: maximum-likelihood two-level random-intercept mixed linear model with
  time as the within-person factor, participant ID as random factor, and sex as
  covariate. Reported estimated means: 2.62 (95% CI 1.98-3.27) at T1 and 1.78
  (1.09-2.47) at T2; p<0.01.
- Exclusions: blind or deaf people and people reporting cognitive impairment;
  practical exclusion also follows from the required internet/device access.
- Secondary measures: total/social loneliness, OSSS-3 social support, PHQ-8,
  GAD-7, EQ-5D-5L, EQ-VAS, attendance, and a single open feedback question.

What was not measured

- No control or counterfactual trajectory.
- No direct observation of attachment relationships, loss of an attachment
  figure, achieved intimacy, or confidence in a specific relationship.
- No independent emotional-loneliness instrument with balanced item keying.
- No item-level T1/T2 results, longitudinal factor model, or measurement-
  invariance test.
- No blinded assessor and no explicit expectancy/demand measure.
- No mediator measure that separates peer support, group contact, CBT content,
  technology learning, facilitator attention, and regression/history effects.
- No long-term follow-up and no established minimal important change for this
  exact subscore in this target setting.
- No probability sample of Barcelona or older adults generally.

Definition-operationalization check

The target defines emotional loneliness as:

  "the absence of one or more attachment figures with whom one could establish
  a close connection, and the desired level of intimacy or confidence is not
  achieved."

The retrieved standard 11-item DJGLS contains the following items (the validated
Spanish form preserves these contents):

  1. There is always someone I can talk to about day-to-day problems. [positive]
  2. I miss having a really close friend. [negative; emotional]
  3. I experience a general sense of emptiness. [negative; emotional]
  4. There are plenty of people I can lean on when I have problems. [positive]
  5. I miss the pleasure of the company of others. [negative; emotional]
  6. I find my circle of friends and acquaintances too limited. [negative; emotional]
  7. There are many people I can trust completely. [positive]
  8. There are enough people I feel close to. [positive]
  9. I miss having people around me. [negative; emotional]
 10. I often feel rejected. [negative; emotional]
 11. I can call on my friends whenever I need them. [positive]

Standard scoring counts "yes" or "more or less" on the six negative items as
loneliness; those six form the emotional subscore. It counts "no" or "more or
less" on the five positive items as loneliness; those five form the social
subscore.

Operationalization retrieval basis: ITEMS-RETRIEVED for the published
11-item instrument and validated Spanish form. The target does not identify its
administered translation, so wording fidelity to that form cannot be verified.

Operationalization scope result: broader.

Specific out-of-definition content:

- Item 3 measures general emptiness, not specifically absence of an attachment
  figure or unmet intimacy/confidence.
- Item 5 measures missing company generally.
- Item 6 measures the perceived size of a friend/acquaintance circle, which the
  target's own introduction assigns to social loneliness.
- Item 9 measures missing people around generally.
- Item 10 measures rejection, without identifying an attachment relationship
  or desired intimacy/confidence.

Item 2 is the closest direct fit. Because the other five emotional-subscale
items extend past the explicit definition, this is a Definition-
operationalization mismatch, subtype definitional scope creep.

Proxy status

The DJGLS subscore is direct evidence of responses to its six items and a proxy
for the paper's attachment/intimacy construct. It is a plausible but non-unique
proxy. In a Spanish older-adult validation, the same six "emotional" items were
exactly the negatively worded items. Two superior models fit those items either
as emotional-specific variance or as a negative-wording method factor, and the
authors explained that the two interpretations cannot be empirically separated
unless content and keying are crossed. All six negative items also had large
discrimination on the second dimension. The scale therefore identifies a
negative-item component, but not uniquely emotional attachment/intimacy.

Pathologization/P2 check

No pathologization finding. The items ask about experiences and relationships;
the selected claim does not grade a belief as false or punish an established-
accurate belief. No specific P2 subclaim exists; strength gate = VOID.

4. LEVEL-OF-ANALYSIS CHECK
--------------------------

Evidence level

- Individual survey respondents.
- Telephone-administered self-report.
- Two measurements over eight weeks.
- Non-probability, technology-access, volunteer pilot sample.
- Model-derived sample means.

Claim level

- Field 6: within-sample change in a person-level emotional-loneliness construct.
- Deferred source claims: causal intervention capability and relevance to older
  vulnerable populations generally.
- Retrieved downstream claims: general loneliness, digital social support,
  social networking/community effects, marginalized groups, and social
  connectedness.

Bridge assumptions for Field 6

1. The six-item score has the same measurement structure at T1 and T2.
2. Change in the negative-item component is change in the emotional-specific
   attachment/intimacy construct rather than response style, generalized
   distress, demand, or broader loneliness.
3. Differential attrition does not materially alter the model's T2 estimand.

These assumptions were implied, not tested. A domain expert could accept the
DJGLS as a common loneliness instrument while still rejecting a uniquely
emotional-facet interpretation without item-level longitudinal or balanced-
keying evidence. There is no person-to-population level error in Field 6 because
the map limits it to the pilot; population and intervention-class applications
add untested bridges.

6. ECOLOGICAL VALIDITY CHECK
----------------------------

The intervention itself was a real eight-week facilitated online group rather
than a laboratory analogue, and the scale was administered in ordinary
telephone interviews. That gives reasonable ecological contact with this
specific program and its volunteers.

The sample does not resemble digitally excluded, sensory-impaired, cognitively
impaired, unwilling, or randomly selected older adults. The immediate post
time-scale does not establish durability. Self-report subscale movement is not
the same object as observed attachment relations, durable intimacy, network
change, or population mental-health outcomes.

Verdict: ecologically valid only for the narrower claim about immediate
post-program scores among observed, technology-access volunteers.


7. DECISIVE-TEST CHECK
----------------------

- Measure: item-level latent change in emotional attachment/intimacy, using a
  measure that crosses emotional/social content with positive/negative keying,
  alongside the DJGLS and a convergent emotional-loneliness instrument.
- Population: a larger sample of older adults recruited beyond current
  technology users, with prespecified subgroup and accessibility plans.
- Controls: randomized waitlist/active control; blinded or independent outcome
  assessors; balanced attention; preregistered missing-data plan; longitudinal
  measurement-invariance testing; and longer follow-up.
- Outcome supporting the source's construct claim: a treatment-by-time change
  on an invariant emotional-specific latent factor, converging across measures
  and separable from negative-keying and general loneliness factors.
- Outcome weakening/falsifying it: change confined to negative-keyed items,
  failure of invariance, no difference from control, or no convergence with a
  balanced emotional-specific measure.
- Current source: does not run this test. It explicitly asks for an RCT but does
  not identify the content/keying or longitudinal-measurement test.

The decisive test would be: Randomize a larger, accessibility-inclusive older-
adult sample to BLOC or an attention-matched control and test preregistered
item-level longitudinal change on a balanced-keyed emotional-attachment factor,
with independent assessment, measurement invariance, convergent measures, and
long-term follow-up.


8. CROSS-REANALYSIS OBJECT CHECK
--------------------------------

N/A for the target dataset. No independent reanalysis or replication of the

14. VERDICT
-----------

- Claim under audit: USEFUL-FOR-NARROWER.
- DEFEATS finding(s): none.
- Failure mechanism: major proxy failure for the emotional-specific
  interpretation; no fatal failure for the scale-score result.
- Surviving residual: Among observed participants, the sex-adjusted estimated
  mean count on the six negatively keyed DJGLS items labeled emotional was 0.84
  points lower at T2 than at T1 (2.62 versus 1.78; p<0.01).
- Residual usefulness grade: C.

The result is numerically inspectable and survives as an instrument-score
statement. It does not survive unchanged as a uniquely emotional attachment/
intimacy construct because the retrieved item content is broader and the six-
item facet is identical to negative keying. Spanish older-adult psychometrics
establish the confound but cannot decide whether the second component is
emotional content, method variance, or both. The proper result is therefore a
materially narrower residual, not wholesale invalidation and not a causal
judgment about BLOC.


15. PAPER-SAFE WORDING
----------------------

A. Neutral summary sentence:

In this non-controlled pilot, the sex-adjusted estimated mean on the six-item
DJGLS subscale labeled emotional loneliness was 0.84 points lower immediately
after BLOC than before it (2.62 versus 1.78 on a 0-6 scale; p<0.01).

B. Direct methodological critique:

The paper interprets a change in six negatively keyed, content-broad DJGLS items
as a change in its narrower attachment/intimacy construct without showing that
emotional content is separable from wording method effects across time.

C. Strong adversarial version:

The 0.84-point result establishes movement in a negative-item bundle, not a
uniquely identified reduction in emotional loneliness, because "emotional"
content and negative wording are perfectly confounded in the instrument.

D. Decisive-test sentence:

An attention-controlled randomized study with item-level longitudinal
invariance and a balanced-keyed, convergent emotional-attachment measure would
show whether BLOC changes emotional loneliness rather than only the DJGLS's
negative-item component.

- DEFEATS finding(s), if any: none.
- Surviving residual: Among observed participants, the sex-adjusted estimated
  mean count on the six negatively keyed DJGLS items labeled emotional was 0.84
  points lower at T2 than at T1 (2.62 versus 1.78; p<0.01).
- Residual usefulness grade: C.
- Strongest defensible claim: the exact surviving residual above.
- Claim being overextended: that the 0.84-point negative-item score difference
  uniquely quantifies a decrease in attachment/intimacy-defined emotional
  loneliness.
- Definition-operationalization mismatch: broader; subtype definitional scope
  creep.
- Construct-establishment gap: none for Field 6; PREVALENCE, FORM, and DOMAIN
  were ESTABLISHED-REPORTED in the map for the selected sample, WRONGNESS was
  inapplicable, and HARM was UNKNOWN but non-load-bearing.
- Grade-before-assert outcome: no specific belief subclaim; P2 VOID.

  negative wording, so the instrument cannot uniquely identify which component
  changed.
- Main level-of-analysis problem: none for the pilot-sample Field 6 claim;
  population/intervention-class extrapolations require untested bridges.
