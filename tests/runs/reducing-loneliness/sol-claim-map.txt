1. WHAT WAS LITERALLY MEASURED
------------------------------

Design, population, sample, and time

- A non-controlled prospective pilot conducted in Barcelona, Spain, from
  October 2021 through January 2022.
- Recruitment was by posters, pamphlets, calls, and presentations through
  primary-care and older-adult centres. Approximately 500 people with the
  relevant profile were offered participation; 63 expressed interest and were
  screened; 27 consented; 21 supplied the post-intervention interview.
- Eligibility required age 65 or older, a stated wish to "connect more and
  better," willingness to participate, and internet plus computer/smartphone
  access. Blindness, deafness, and self-reported cognitive impairment were
  exclusions.
- Baseline N = 27; mean age 74.26 years (range 66-88); 74% women. Post N = 21;
  reported dropout = 22%.
- Participants attended eight weekly Zoom sessions of 90-120 minutes in groups
  of 6-8, led by two gerontologist facilitators. The intervention combined a
  community/social-activity component with peer support, cognitive-behavioural
  activities, coping strategies, reminiscence, and purpose-in-life work.
- Outcomes were elicited by telephone before and after the eight-week program
  by two trained members of the research team.
- Unit of analysis: participant repeated measures. The paper fitted random-
  intercept mixed linear models with time as the repeated factor, participant
  ID as a random factor, and sex as a covariate.

Primary outcome used for the selected claim

- The named instrument was the 11-item De Jong Gierveld Loneliness Scale
  (DJGLS): total score 0-11, social subscore 0-5, and emotional subscore 0-6.
- The target article does not print the items, state the administered language,
  identify a translation, or report item-level responses. It reports an
  emotional-subscale alpha of 0.7409 in S2 Table.
- Table 2 reports sex-adjusted estimated means for the emotional subscale of
  2.62 (95% CI 1.98-3.27) at T1 and 1.78 (1.09-2.47) at T2, a difference of
  0.84 points, p<0.01. For comparison, total loneliness was 4.32 versus 3.86,
  p=0.218, and social loneliness was 1.70 versus 2.03, p=0.128.

Definition-operationalization check

The source explicitly defines the construct as follows:

  "Emotional loneliness refers to the absence of one or more attachment
  figures with whom one could establish a close connection, and the desired
  level of intimacy or confidence is not achieved." (Introduction)

The standard 11-item form and the validated Spanish wording were retrieved from
the original scale record and Tomás et al. (2017), Table 5. The target paper's
exact administered translation remains unreported. The six items assigned to
the emotional subscale are the negatively worded items:

  2. "I miss having a really close friend."
  3. "I experience a general sense of emptiness."
  5. "I miss the pleasure of the company of others."
  6. "I find my circle of friends and acquaintances too limited."
  9. "I miss having people around me."
 10. "I often feel rejected."

The other five, positively worded items are assigned to the social subscale:

  1. "There is always someone I can talk to about my day-to-day problems."
  4. "There are plenty of people I can lean on when I have problems."
  7. "There are many people I can trust completely."
  8. "There are enough people I feel close [to]."
 11. "I can call on my friends whenever I need [them]."

The standard coding treats "yes" and "more or less" on negative items as
loneliness and "no" and "more or less" on positive items as loneliness. Thus,
the emotional score is also exactly the count of six negatively keyed items.

Operationalization retrieval basis: ITEMS-RETRIEVED. This applies to the
published 11-item DJGLS and its validated Spanish form; the target's exact
administered translation was not supplied.

Operationalization scope result: broader.

The definition is specifically about missing an attachment figure and unmet
intimacy/confidence. Items 3 (general emptiness), 5 (company), 6 (limited circle
of friends/acquaintances), 9 (people around), and 10 (rejection) extend beyond
that stated attachment/intimacy definition. Item 2 is the closest direct match.

Map content: Definition-operationalization mismatch.
Subtype: definitional scope creep.

2. STRONGEST CLAIM THE SOURCE ITSELF MAKES
------------------------------------------

The source moves among three levels while preserving some hedges:

- Results, quantified: "Emotional loneliness decreased by 0.84 points on a
  scale of 0 to 6" (Table 2 narrative; p<0.01).
- Discussion, stronger description: participants showed a "substantial
  reduction in emotional loneliness" following the intervention.
- Conclusion, causal-purpose wording with a feasibility hedge: "The present
  pilot study tested a promising online psychology tool to reduce emotional
  loneliness and depressive symptoms." The abstract/conclusion also states
  that online interventions for vulnerable populations "can become essential"
  for lessening pandemic consequences.

The methods and limitations supply a countervailing hedge: the authors call the
study a feasibility, non-controlled pilot and state that the absence of a
control group limits attribution of results to the intervention; the results
are preliminary and require an RCT.


3. WEAKER DEFENSIBLE CLAIM THE EVIDENCE ACTUALLY SUPPORTS
---------------------------------------------------------

Author reconstruction from Field 1:

Among the observed participants in this self-selected Barcelona pilot, the
sex-adjusted estimated mean count on the six negatively keyed DJGLS items that
the instrument labels "emotional loneliness" was 0.84 points lower at the
post-intervention interview than at baseline (2.62 versus 1.78 on a 0-6 scale;
p<0.01); this is a within-sample temporal score difference, not by itself an
estimate of the intervention's causal effect or of change in a uniquely
identified attachment/intimacy construct.

This reconstruction tracks the retrieved items because the operationalization
is broader than the source's definition.

5A. Within-source component

The first transition occurs in the paper:

1. Instrument score -> named latent construct. Table 2 contains a change in the
   six-item negative-keyed subscore, while the narrative states that "emotional
   loneliness decreased by 0.84 points." Relative to the paper's own narrower
   attachment/intimacy definition, this removes the broader item content and
   the fact that subscale membership is identical to negative wording.

2. Temporal difference -> intervention capability. The conclusion moves from a
   non-controlled T1/T2 difference to a "promising online psychology tool to
   reduce" the outcomes, while the limitations state that attribution is not
   possible without a control group.

Failure location: PAPER. Verification state: FULL-TEXT-CONFIRMED.

5B. Downstream component

- D3 changes a significant emotional-subscale result into significant
  "loneliness" generally, then adds a protective-effect interpretation for
  digital social support despite null total-loneliness, social-loneliness, and
  social-support results. Failure location: CITATION.
- D4 changes a facilitator-led Zoom intervention into evidence about social-
  networking platforms and online communities facilitating social connectedness
  for older and marginalized groups. Failure location: CITATION.
- D1 is an accurate high-level method classification and does not add a failure
  location. D2 largely reproduces the source's own framing; any causal-purpose
