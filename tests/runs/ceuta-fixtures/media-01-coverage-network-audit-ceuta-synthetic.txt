WHAT THIS AUDIT CANNOT DO — RESTATED AS REQUIRED:
It maps how a story propagated and where framings differ. It does NOT establish
which framing is correct. Distribution across outlets is a distribution, not a
verdict. Majority coverage is not evidence of accuracy — least of all here, where
the quantitative core traces to one upstream origin. Adjudicating the underlying
facts requires primary material, which is downstream of this audit and human.

SCOPE LIMITS — RESTATED
=================================================================

1. Coverage distribution is not a truth verdict. Majority framing is not evidence
   of accuracy, and here the majority shares one upstream origin for every number
   in the story (8 ÷ 1). Eight outlets reporting a figure is one source reported
   eight times.

2. One event does not establish an outlet's practice. Nothing above is a claim
   about how Reuters, AP, Al Jazeera, the BBC, France 24, NPR, CBC or the New York
   Times generally behave. Establishing that requires a corpus across events — a
   different and much larger audit.

3. Divergence does not establish intent. Editorial choice is observable in the
   text; motive is not, and none is imputed here.

4. Wire dependence, length, deadline and genre explain substantial variance. Wire
   dependence was tested and turned out to be the main explanation for the
   clustering. Length could NOT be tested and is the largest open variable.

5. A convenience sample supports findings about the pieces examined, not about
   coverage generally. This sample is convenience-grade and additionally inherits
   an unknown prior-run selection.

6. **FIXTURE LIMIT, ADDED FOR THIS RUN.** The pieces are synthetic. Findings are
   about a constructed artifact bearing real mastheads, and the fixture's own
   instruction to relabel outlets A–H before publication should be followed. The
   paraphrasing fixture also disables the verbatim-overlap test, which is normally
   this branch's strongest evidence, and supplies no primary source in either form,
   which the prompt itself identifies as making verb divergence uninterpretable.
   A live run would be materially stronger on both counts.
