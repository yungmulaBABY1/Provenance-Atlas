SOURCE OF FAILURE

An ISIS-2 (1988) calibration audit returned Verdict A with no declared source
retrieval basis and no verification-state declarations for its specific figures.
Independent checking later found that four of five figures were source-consistent.
The combination effect was reported in the audit as approximately 40 percent;
the retrieved trial figure was 42 percent ± 5. The analytical failure is not that
the acquittal was necessarily wrong. It is that the output did not distinguish
retrieval-grounded accuracy from confident recall.

PATCHED TEST CONDITION

Rerun the same ISIS-2 target using the patched construct-validity prompt and the
same declared target claim. Do not supply the prior verdict as an answer key.

EXPECTED RESULT — LOCKED BEFORE RERUN

1. Before Section 1, the audit states an explicit `SOURCE RETRIEVAL BASIS:` line,
   regardless of its eventual verdict.

2. Every specific load-bearing figure quoted — including dosing, sample size,
   percentages, effect size, and NNT — carries an individual verification state
   and source, inline or in the required compact table.

3. The combination odds-reduction figure is handled in one of these inspectable
   ways:

   - source-accurate 42 percent ± 5 is reported;
   - approximately 40 percent is explicitly identified as a rounded summary of
     the retrieved 42 percent ± 5 figure; or
   - the figure is marked PROVISIONAL / recalled-not-verified when it was not
     independently checked.

4. The overall verdict may remain A. This preflight tests whether the evidentiary
   basis is inspectable; it is not designed to force an adverse conclusion.

FAILURE MEANING

- No retrieval-basis line despite Verdict A: Rule 6's empty-table path improperly
  waived the unconditional preflight.
- Retrieval-basis line present but figures remain uncategorized individually:
  the per-figure requirement did not execute.
- Approximately 40 percent appears without a retrieved 42 percent ± 5 source,
  an explicit rounding relationship, or a PROVISIONAL flag: the declaration was
  treated as ceremony rather than a retrieval discipline.
